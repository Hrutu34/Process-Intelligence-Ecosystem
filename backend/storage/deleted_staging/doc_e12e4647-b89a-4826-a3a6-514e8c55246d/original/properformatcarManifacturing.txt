# Car Manufacturing Process – Process Analysis Document

## 1. Process Overview

**Process Name:** Car Manufacturing
**Process Objective:** Convert raw materials and purchased components into a completed, tested, and approved vehicle ready for dispatch.

### Process Flow

**Product Planning → Engineering → Supplier Procurement → Incoming Inspection → Stamping → Body Shop → Paint Shop → Powertrain → Final Assembly → Testing → Quality Release → Dispatch**

# 6. Decisions

Decisions determine which path the process should follow.

### Decision 1 – Is the material acceptable?

**Yes →** Release material to production
**No →** Quarantine material and initiate supplier/non-conformance process

### Decision 2 – Is the stamped panel within specification?

**Yes →** Send to body shop
**No →** Reject/rework panel

### Decision 3 – Is the body dimensionally correct?

**Yes →** Send to paint shop
**No →** Correct/rework body

### Decision 4 – Is paint quality acceptable?

**Yes →** Send to assembly
**No →** Paint repair/rework

### Decision 5 – Is assembly complete?

**Yes →** Continue to testing
**No →** Return to relevant assembly station

### Decision 6 – Does the vehicle pass diagnostic testing?

**Yes →** Continue final inspection
**No →** Troubleshoot and repair

### Decision 7 – Does the vehicle pass final quality inspection?

**Yes →** Release vehicle
**No →** Send for rework

### Decision 8 – Is the vehicle approved for dispatch?

**Yes →** Dispatch
**No →** Hold vehicle until requirements are satisfied

# 7. Conflicts

Conflicts are situations where different requirements, teams, systems, or process conditions compete with each other.

### Common Manufacturing Conflicts

| Conflict                     | Example                                             | Potential Impact              |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Production vs Quality        | Production wants to continue despite defects        | Quality risk                  |
| Production vs Maintenance    | Equipment requires maintenance during production    | Production downtime           |
| Cost vs Quality              | Lower-cost component may have higher quality risk   | Customer complaints           |
| Delivery vs Quality          | Shipment deadline conflicts with unresolved defects | Unsafe/non-conforming vehicle |
| Supplier vs Manufacturer     | Supplier disputes responsibility for defective part | Production disruption         |
| Engineering vs Manufacturing | Design is difficult to manufacture                  | Increased cost/time           |
| Inventory vs Production      | Required component unavailable                      | Line stoppage                 |
| Manual vs Automated Process  | Automation failure requires manual intervention     | Reduced productivity          |
| Software vs Hardware         | ECU/software incompatibility                        | Vehicle test failure          |
| Model Mix vs Line Capacity   | Multiple variants require different operations      | Line imbalance                |

### Conflict Resolution

Typical resolution steps are:

**Conflict Identified → Containment → Investigation → Root Cause Analysis → Decision → Corrective Action → Verification**

# 12. Exception Handling

The manufacturing process must also define what happens when something goes wrong.

## Example: Defective Component

**Defect Detected**
↓
**Stop/Contain Affected Material**
↓
**Identify Part and Supplier**
↓
**Record Non-Conformance**
↓
**Investigate Root Cause**
↓
**Decide: Rework / Return / Scrap / Use-As-Is if formally approved**
↓
**Verify Corrective Action**
↓
**Release Material**

---

# 13. Example: Vehicle Fails Final Inspection

**Vehicle Fails Inspection**
↓
**Create Defect Record**
↓
**Identify Responsible Process**
↓
**Move Vehicle to Rework Area**
↓
**Perform Approved Repair**
↓
**Repeat Required Inspection/Test**
↓
**Decision: Pass?**

**Yes → Final Approval → Dispatch**

**No → Additional Investigation/Rework**

The process ends when:

# 17. Final Process Definition

**Car manufacturing is a controlled, multi-actor, multi-system process in which materials, engineering information, people, machines, and software interact through a sequence of activities. Events trigger or interrupt activities, decisions determine process paths, and conflicts are managed through defined controls and escalation procedures. The final objective is to produce a vehicle that satisfies engineering, quality, safety, regulatory, and customer requirements before release and dispatch.**
